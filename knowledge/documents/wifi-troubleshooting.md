## Symptoms
- The device shows no Wi-Fi networks, or the company network is missing from the list.
- The device connects to Wi-Fi but has no internet access.
- The connection is very slow or drops repeatedly.
- The device repeatedly asks for a Wi-Fi password or credentials.

## Common Causes
- Wi-Fi is switched off, or airplane mode is on.
- The user is far from an access point or has a weak signal.
- A temporary fault on the device's wireless adapter.
- Saved network credentials are out of date, for example after a password change.
- A wider outage affecting the office network or a home internet connection.

## Troubleshooting Steps
1. Check that Wi-Fi is turned on and airplane mode is off.
2. Check the signal strength. Move closer to the access point or router and see if the connection improves.
3. Turn Wi-Fi off, wait 10 seconds, and turn it on again, then reconnect to the network.
4. If the device asks for credentials, sign in again. If the company password was changed recently, remove the saved network and reconnect with the new password.
5. Check whether other devices on the same network can reach the internet. If none can, the problem is probably the network or the internet provider, not the laptop.
6. Restart the device.
7. On the office network, check whether colleagues nearby have the same problem and note the location and time.
8. Record the results in the ticket, including the network name, the location and the error message, if any.

## Restricted Actions
- Do not change router, access point or firewall settings.
- Do not ask the user to connect to unknown or open networks to work around the problem.
- Do not ask the user to turn off security software.

## When to Escalate
Escalate to the Network team if:
- several users in the same area are affected;
- the device cannot see the company network but other devices can;
- the problem continues after the steps above on more than one network.

Escalate to Hardware / IT Support if the device does not detect any wireless networks at all.
